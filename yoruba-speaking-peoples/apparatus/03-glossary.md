# Glossary of Yoruba Deities, Customs, and Terms

This glossary cross-references Ellis’s nineteenth-century transliterations with modern standardized Yoruba terms.

### Deities and Spiritual Entities (*Orishas*)
- **Olorun**: "Owner of Heaven"; the Supreme Being in traditional Yoruba cosmology.
- **Obatala (Orishanla)**: The deity of white cloth, purity, creation, and ethics; regarded as the sculptor of human physical form.
- **Odudua (Oduduwa)**: In Ellis’s account, represented as the female counterpart of Obatala and goddess of the earth; in traditional Oyo-Ife historiography, the legendary patriarch and founding ruler of the Yoruba royal dynasties.
- **Shango (Sango)**: Deified monarch of Oyo; Orisha of thunder, lightning, fire, and divine retribution.
- **Ogun**: The Orisha of iron, war, hunting, and technological craft.
- **Oshun (Osun)**: River deity associated with fertility, beauty, maternal care, and fresh waters.
- **Ifa (Orunmila)**: The deity and sacred system of divination, destiny, and cosmic wisdom.
- **Eshu (Elegbara)**: The divine messenger, guardian of the crossroads, and agent of moral testing and order.
- **Oya**: River goddess and fierce deity of the tempest, wind, and lightning; principal consort of Sango.

### Sacred and Social Customs
- **Babalawo**: Diviner and master of the sixteen principal Odus of the Ifa system.
- **Opon Ifa**: The sacred circular or rectangular tray used during divination.
- **Opele**: The consecrated chain cast during divination consultations to reveal Odu signatures.
- **Egungun**: Ancestral masquerade honoring deceased lineage forebears.
- **Oro**: Ancient sacred society performing nocturnal judicial and purification rites.
- **Ila**: Traditional facial scarification patterns denoting lineage, ethnic sub-group, or social affiliation.
