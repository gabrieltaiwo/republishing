## Description (HTML for Amazon)
<p><strong>A foundational 19th-century anthropological survey of Yoruba religion, mythology, sacred priesthoods, and customary law.</strong></p>

<p>First published in London in 1894 by Lieutenant-Colonel A. B. Ellis, <em>The Yoruba-Speaking Peoples of the Slave Coast of West Africa</em> is one of the earliest comprehensive European studies of Yoruba civilization. Operating along the West African coast at the close of the nineteenth century, Ellis recorded in granular detail the religious pantheon, sacred priesthoods, legal traditions, and oral literature of the Yoruba people before the full consolidation of British colonial rule.</p>

<p>The work provides an extraordinary record of the Yoruba Orisha pantheon—including detailed chapters dedicated to Olorun, Obatala, Odudua, Shango, Ogun, Oshun, and Ifa—alongside an extensive study of the Ifa divination system, secret societies (Oro and Egungun), land tenure, marriage customs, and over 250 traditional proverbs.</p>

<p><strong>This Modern Annotated Edition Features:</strong></p>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Fully Cleaned and Restored Text:</strong> Carefully reformatted from original 1894 first editions for seamless reading in print and digital formats.</li>
    <li><strong>Critical Modern Introduction:</strong> A scholarly opening essay analyzing Ellis's late-Victorian methodology, examining his strengths, limitations, and how his coastal survey pairs with Samuel Johnson's <em>History of the Yorubas</em>.</li>
    <li><strong>Comprehensive Orisha and Cultural Glossary:</strong> Standardized definitions cross-referencing Ellis's 19th-century anglicized spellings with modern Yoruba orthography.</li>
    <li><strong>Complete Original Proverbs & Folklore:</strong> The full compendium of 250+ Yoruba proverbs and nature myths preserved with original English translations.</li>
    <li><strong>Scholarly Note on the Text:</strong> Archival documentation detailing source origins and editorial policy.</li>
</ul>

<p><em>An indispensable companion volume for historians, practitioners of traditional African religions, and students of the African diaspora.</em></p>
