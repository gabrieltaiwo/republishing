## Description (HTML for Amazon)
<p><strong>The definitive, restored edition of the greatest historical masterpiece on Yoruba civilization and West African statecraft.</strong></p>

<p>Written in the late 1890s by Reverend Samuel Johnson—a native Yoruba scholar whose lineage traced directly to the royal house of Oyo—<em>The History of the Yorubas</em> is an unmatched epic of African memory. Drawing directly from the royal bards (<em>Arokin</em>), councilors of state, and battlefield commanders, Johnson chronicled the entire trajectory of the Yoruba people: from legendary origins under Oduduwa at Ile-Ife, through the rise and zenith of imperial Oyo, to the dramatic nineteenth-century civil wars and the dawn of the colonial protectorate.</p>

<p>Far more than a simple chronicle of kings, this monumental volume provides an exhaustive encyclopedia of Yoruba life, detailing social institutions, legal systems, military strategies, royal rituals, religious cosmology, proverbs, and poetry.</p>

<p><strong>This Modern Annotated Edition Features:</strong></p>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Complete, Restored Text:</strong> Meticulously typeset and cleaned from original 1921 first-edition scans to eliminate historical scanning defects and OCR errors while honoring Johnson's original prose.</li>
    <li><strong>Scholarly Historical Introduction:</strong> Critical background on Johnson’s remarkable life, the loss and reconstruction of the manuscript, and the book's lasting historiographical importance.</li>
    <li><strong>Comprehensive Cultural Glossary:</strong> Clear definitions for classical political offices (Alaafin, Bashorun, Kakanfo), sacred deities (Orishas), and institutional terms.</li>
    <li><strong>Note on the Text:</strong> Complete documentation of original source editions and editorial principles.</li>
    <li><strong>Complete Original Footnotes:</strong> Every authorial and editorial annotation preserved in full.</li>
</ul>

<p><em>Essential reading for historians, anthropologists, students of the African diaspora, and anyone seeking to understand the deep roots of West African culture.</em></p>
