# Introduction to the Annotated Edition

The Reverend Samuel Johnson’s *The History of the Yorubas* stands as one of the most remarkable intellectual achievements of early twentieth-century African historiography. Written in the late 1890s and finally published in London in 1921 after an extraordinary journey of loss, re-creation, and editorial rescue, it remains the foundational anchor for any serious study of Yoruba civilization, statecraft, religion, and literature.

## The Author and His Mission

Born in Hastings, Sierra Leone in 1846 to recaptive Yoruba parents who traced their lineage back to the royal house of Oyo, Samuel Johnson returned to his ancestral homeland as a young missionary schoolmaster and catechist under the Church Missionary Society (CMS). Based for decades in Oyo and later in Ibadan, Johnson found himself not only an evangelist but also a trusted diplomatic intermediary during the protracted Yoruba Civil Wars (the Kiriji / Ekitiparapo Wars) of the late nineteenth century. 

Recognizing that the oral traditions, sacred chants, and historical memories of the Yoruba elders were vulnerable to dissolution under colonial partition and rapid modernization, Johnson set out to preserve them. Relying directly on the Royal Bards (*Arokin*) of the Alaafin of Oyo, councilors of state, and provincial warlords, he produced a comprehensive history of an African nation from its legendary genesis to the establishment of the British Protectorate.

Tragically, Johnson’s original manuscript, dispatched to England in 1899, was misplaced by the publisher. With legendary resolve, Johnson painstakingly reconstructed the entire work from his personal field notes before his death in 1901. His brother, Dr. Obadiah Johnson, subsequently prepared and edited the recovered manuscript for its landmark 1921 publication.

## Historical and Cultural Significance

Johnson’s work divides naturally into two parts:
1. **The People, Their Country, and Customs**: A rigorous anthropological survey detailing Yoruba ethnology, grammar, cosmology, royal rituals, military apparatus, law, and social institutions.
2. **The Dynastic Chronicle**: A comprehensive sequential account of the kings of Oyo (*Alaafins*), beginning with Oduduwa and Oranyan, tracing the zenith of imperial Oyo, its collapse in the early nineteenth century, and the tumultuous civil conflicts that followed.

While Johnson’s perspective is inevitably influenced by his Victorian missionary upbringing and an unabashedly Oyo-centric vantage point, his record preserves names, genealogies, proverbs, and poetry that would otherwise have vanished from human memory.

## About This Restored Edition

This new critical edition restores Johnson’s monumental text to modern readers with careful attention to readability and cultural fidelity:
- **Cleaned and Restored Text**: Hyphenation breaks, running headers, and mechanical typesetting errors from the 1921 first edition have been corrected while strictly preserving Johnson’s original prose and orthography.
- **Critical Apparatus**: An expanded glossary of classical Yoruba political titles, sacred concepts, and historical locations accompanies the text to assist modern readers, diaspora researchers, and students.
- **Editorial Policy**: All original footnotes and historical appendices have been retained in full.
