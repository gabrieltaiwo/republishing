## Description (HTML for Amazon)
<p><strong>A classic treasury of forty timeless West African folktales, trickster fables, and nature myths from the historic Cross River and Niger Delta regions.</strong></p>

<p>First recorded in 1910 by District Commissioner Elphinstone Dayrell, with an introduction by renowned folklorist Andrew Lang, <em>Folk Stories from Southern Nigeria</em> offers a captivating window into the rich oral storytelling traditions of West Africa. Gathered directly from indigenous storytellers, elders, and local chiefs, these forty enchanting tales reflect the wisdom, humor, morality, and cosmological imagination of southern Nigerian communities.</p>

<p>Inside this collection, readers will discover world-famous origin stories such as <em>Why the Sun and the Moon Live in the Sky</em>, <em>Why the Bat Flies by Night</em>, and <em>Why the Cat Kills Rats</em>, alongside cunning adventures of the Tortoise—the legendary West African trickster—and dramatic legends of sacred drums, spirit transformations, and ancient kings.</p>

<p><strong>This Modern Annotated Edition Features:</strong></p>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Fully Cleaned and Restored Text:</strong> Formatted for optimal readability across modern Kindle and paperback editions.</li>
    <li><strong>Scholarly Modern Introduction:</strong> A new introductory essay analyzing the cultural functions of West African oral folklore, the trickster archetype, and the shared mythological themes uniting Yoruba, Efik, and Igbo storytelling.</li>
    <li><strong>Comprehensive Folklore Glossary:</strong> Explanatory definitions of indigenous concepts, traditional currencies (manillas and brass rods), and regional customs.</li>
    <li><strong>The Original Andrew Lang Essay:</strong> Complete historical introduction by one of the founders of comparative folklore.</li>
    <li><strong>Scholarly Note on the Text:</strong> Archival documentation detailing the 1910 first edition and editorial methodology.</li>
</ul>

<p><em>A delightful and enlightening read for families, educators, folklorists, and anyone drawn to the vibrant heritage of African storytelling.</em></p>
