# Glossary of Folklore, Cultural Terms, and Regional Idioms

This glossary defines regional Nigerian idioms, chiefdom customs, and historical currency appearing in *Folk Stories from Southern Nigeria*.

### Cultural Concepts and Folklore Motifs
- **The Tortoise (Ijapa / Ikun)**: The archetypal trickster figure in southern Nigerian and West African folklore; small and slow, but possessing unmatched cunning, resourcefulness, and opportunistic greed.
- **Ju-Ju (Juju)**: A West African term referring to sacred amulets, spiritual charms, indigenous shrines, or spiritual power mediating human affairs and oaths.
- **Play (Native Play)**: A communal village gathering featuring drumming, masked dancing, storytelling, and social celebration.
- **Palaver**: A traditional West African conference, public deliberation, dispute mediation, or community council meeting presided over by chiefs and elders.
- **Tombo (Palm Wine)**: Freshly tapped sap from the oil palm or raffia palm tree, central to hospitality, libations, and ceremonial gatherings.
- **Calabar**: Historic trading city-state and port along the Cross River estuary in southeastern Nigeria.

### Traditional Currency and Trade Goods
- **Manilla**: A horseshoe-shaped ring of bronze, copper, or brass used as a standard medium of commercial exchange across the Niger Delta and Calabar coast from the sixteenth through early twentieth centuries.
- **Brass Rods**: Traditional rods of bent brass utilized alongside manillas and cowrie shells as regional currency.
- **Cloth Money**: Measures of hand-woven cotton cloth historically accepted as units of legal tender and ceremonial dowry payments.
