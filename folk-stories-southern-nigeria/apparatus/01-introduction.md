# Introduction to the Annotated Edition

Published in London in 1910 by Longmans, Green and Co., *Folk Stories from Southern Nigeria, West Africa* is one of the foundational collections of indigenous oral narratives from the Cross River and Niger Delta regions of West Africa. Compiled by Elphinstone Dayrell (1869–1917), a British District Commissioner in Southern Nigeria, with an appreciative opening essay by the preeminent Scottish folklorist Andrew Lang (1844–1912), the anthology preserves forty traditional tales collected directly from native storytellers, chiefs, and local elders.

## The Nature of the Tales

West African storytelling is never mere amusement; it functions as a dynamic instrument of ethical philosophy, social socialization, natural observation, and legal education. The forty narratives in this collection broadly encompass three major categories:

1. **Etiological and "Just-So" Myths**: Stories explaining natural phenomena, animal characteristics, and the physical world (e.g., *Why the Sun and the Moon Live in the Sky*, *Why the Bat Flies by Night*, *Why the Hippopotamus Lives in the Water*).
2. **Trickster and Wisdom Fables**: Tales highlighting cunning, deception, justice, and retribution, prominently featuring the Tortoise—the quintessential West African trickster whose razor-sharp intellect contrasts with the brute strength of leopards, elephants, and bush-cows.
3. **Moral and Chieftaincy Legends**: Allegorical narratives examining greed, fidelity, hospitality, and the dangers of tyranny, often centered on kings, magic ju-ju drums, wrestling contests, and spirit ancestors.

## Cultural and Regional Heritage

While recorded along the Calabar and Cross River administrative districts (capturing primarily Efik, Ibibio, and neighboring southern Nigerian oral traditions), these tales share deep mythological affinities with Yoruba, Igbo, and wider West African folklore. The themes of ancestral spirits, sacred trees, spirit transformations, and trickster wit represent the shared cultural bedrock of the Bight of Benin and Biafra.

## About This Restored Edition

This modern annotated edition brings these timeless oral treasures to contemporary readers, educators, and storytellers:
- **Cleaned and Formatted Prose**: Faithfully formatted from the 1910 first edition, removing typesetting anomalies while preserving Dayrell’s direct narrative cadence.
- **Folklore and Cultural Glossary**: Explanatory notes on regional terms, sacred shrines, traditional currency (*manillas*, brass rods), and chiefdom institutions.
- **Andrew Lang’s Classic Introduction**: Preserving the historical framing provided by one of the pioneers of comparative folklore.
